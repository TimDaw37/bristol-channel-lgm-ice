    Reading the margins

    • Three source families, not five independent tests. PaleoMIST 1.0 takes its Eurasian margins from the DATED-1 most-credible lines, with intermediate slices interpolated. Batchelor et al. is a hemispheric compilation of published outlines. BRITICE-CHRONO supplies both an empirical reconstruction and an ice-sheet model nudged towards it.
    • Bounds are not predictions. BRITICE-CHRONO and DATED-1 each publish a minimum, a preferred and a maximum extent. The minimum and maximum are the authors' uncertainty bounds on the preferred line. In this sector the BRITICE-CHRONO maximum follows the present shoreline.
    • Chronologies differ. DATED-1 has a census date of 1 January 2013 and places the Celtic Sea lobe in its 23 ka slice. BRITICE-CHRONO, with its later dating programme, places the greatest extent in this map area at 26 ka.
    • Ice on Dartmoor and Exmoor in the model is local. The modelled ice on the South-West peninsula forms as separate upland ice and is not connected to the Irish Sea Ice Stream in any time-slice.
    • Distances are to the modern coast by design. The modern shoreline is where the onshore evidence begins and is the line the maximum bound follows. It was not the shoreline of the time.
    • The shoreline of the time moved with the ice load. In the BRITICE-CHRONO glacio-isostatic palaeotopography the shoreline lies a median 47 km from the modern north coast at 31 ka. Crustal depression under the ice brings it to a median 19 km at 25 ka, and it withdraws to 52 km by 19 ka. At no date does it come within 5 km of the coast measured here. A uniform lowering of sea level by 120 m would not reproduce this.

    Method

    Ice-extent polygons were read from each publication's deposited GIS data, reprojected to a transverse Mercator projection on WGS84 (origin 51°N 5°W, scale factor 1) and clipped to the map area. The BRITICE-CHRONO model margin is the outline of grounded-ice cells in the deposited mask grids, which have a cell size of about 2.5 km, and is drawn unsmoothed. Other margins are simplified with a 250 m tolerance for display; all distances were computed before simplification.

    The coast is the GSHHG full-resolution shoreline. For the profile, the mainland outline was closed across inlets narrower than 3 km and generalised to 400 m, so that the Taw–Torridge, Camel and Hayle estuaries do not register as coast. Distances are planar, from a point to the nearest part of an ice polygon; a point inside a polygon is given its distance to the polygon edge as a negative value. Ice bodies lying wholly on the peninsula, which occur only in the model (Dartmoor and Exmoor), are left out of the coast profile so that it measures ice arriving from the sea; they are included in the distances at named places. Maximum reach is the union of every time-slice of a given margin. The shoreline of the time is the zero contour of the BRITICE-CHRONO palaeotopography grids (500 m cells, lightly smoothed), one per thousand years; shading marks present sea floor that stood above sea level. It is an output of the glacio-isostatic model tied to the BRITICE-CHRONO ice history, so beside the DATED-1, Batchelor and PaleoMIST margins it is indicative only. The land-or-water test samples each ice front every kilometre. Land area under ice is measured on mainland Cornwall, Devon and west Somerset ( km²).

    Line-work in regional and hemispheric compilations is drawn at small scale. Differences of a few kilometres between margins, and between a margin and the coast, are within drafting precision and should not be read as geological statements.
